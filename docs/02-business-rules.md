**Decision outcomes** (screening results arrive by API; until the screening company is connected, the office enters each adult's results). As built: the lowest credit score in the household sets the deposit tier, household incomes combine, income alone never triggers an adverse action notice (it isn't from a consumer report), and every adult pays their own application fee while the main applicant also pays the admin fee:
1. Approve — 620+, income ≥ 2.5×, no eviction in 5 yrs, no landlord balance, no utility/car-payment problems → 1-month deposit; lease goes to e-sign.
2. Approve with higher deposit — under 620, everything else passes → 1.5×–2× (person confirms). **Adverse action notice required (law, FCRA).**

4. Decline — income under 2.5×, eviction in 5 yrs, or landlord balance → person reviews, then adverse action notice **(law)** and $85 admin fee refunded.

## Maintenance priority and emergencies
- Priority comes from triage answers (`docs/03-maintenance-triage.json`), never from the tenant choosing "emergency".
- **AC is an emergency** when the inside temperature is **above 80°F** (summer) **or** young children or elderly people live in the home. This applies whether the AC is blowing warm air or won't turn on at all. Otherwise AC outage is Urgent.
- Other emergency rules are in the triage file (active leaks that can't be stopped, water near electrical, sewage backup, smoke/fire, hot or sparking electrical, door that can't be locked, no heat below 55°F, toilet overflow that the valve doesn't stop, water heater flood).
- Gas smell and active smoke/fire: two buttons, "I smell gas" and "I see smoke, fire or sparks", are always on the first screen of a request. They show safety steps (leave, call 911) immediately and create an Emergency work order with **no questions, no required photos and no false-emergency charge acknowledgment** (never discourage reporting danger).
- **False-emergency charge:** for other emergencies, the tenant must acknowledge before submitting: "My answers are accurate. If this turns out not to be an emergency, I agree to pay the vendor's emergency charge plus a **$100** fee." Store the acknowledgment (timestamp, text shown, answers). The charge must also be in the lease or a maintenance addendum to be enforceable (company's attorney to confirm wording).
- Entry: Florida requires at least **24 hours' notice** for repairs, with entry between **7:30 AM and 8:00 PM** **(law, Fla. Stat. 83.53(2), raised from 12 hours by the 2022 "Miya's Law"; attorney to confirm)**. Send automatically when a vendor schedules, and don't let a vendor pick a time sooner than the notice period or outside those hours. No notice is needed in an emergency or when the tenant agrees to the time. Store the notice period as a company setting (default 24 hours) so it can follow future law changes or other states.
- Entry notice delivery: chapter 83 doesn't say how the 24-hour repair notice must be given. Each scheduled visit records the method (posted on the door, handed to the tenant, mailed, or e-mailed). E-mail counts as the notice only when the tenant signed the 83.505 e-mail notice addendum (lease.email_notices_agreed); otherwise the e-mail to tenants is a courtesy copy.
- Vendor paperwork: a vendor who works on site (trades, cleaning, landscaping, pest, fire, elevator, or trade not set) needs a **W-9 on file and a current liability insurance certificate** before a work order can be sent. Expired workers' comp also blocks. Insurance ending within 30 days shows a warning but still allows dispatch. Utilities, suppliers, government, legal, bookkeeping and media vendors are exempt. Only an admin can allow a specific vendor to be dispatched anyway.
- Vendor paperwork requests: "Request paperwork" (one vendor, or every vendor missing paperwork) e-mails the vendor a private link to upload their W-9, liability insurance certificate (with the end date) and workers' comp certificate or exemption, from a phone, without an account. Reminders go out every 3 days (up to 30) saying they aren't set up and can't be sent work or paid until it's in; the office gets an e-mail when it's complete. Each send makes a new link (only a hash is stored). A 1099 vendor without a W-9 can't be paid (IRS backup withholding).

- Vendor job link: assigning a vendor e-mails them a private link (a new one each assignment) to accept or decline, pick a visit time, and mark the job done with photos and their invoice. A non-emergency visit in an occupied unit must start at least 24 hours after they schedule and fall between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. (83.53(2)); the tenant is e-mailed at once. That e-mail is the legal notice only if the tenant signed the 83.505 e-mail addendum; otherwise the office is told to post or hand-deliver the notice, and the work order shows it as still to do.
- Bills: a vendor's invoice becomes a bill that needs approval (rule 4). Approving posts the expense and the payable to the property owner's books; recording payment posts the payable against operating cash. A 1099 vendor without a W-9 can't be paid. Voiding an approved bill reverses its entry; paid bills can't be voided.

## Rent, fees and payments
- Lease lengths: SIM REI offers 7 to 24 months (Florida ch. 83 sets no maximum; leases over a year must be written and signed, and witnesses are not required for leases, Fla. Stat. 689.01(1)). Under 12 months adds the short-term surcharge; over 12 months the lease states a yearly rent increase (SIM REI: 3%), since a fixed-term lease can't be changed mid-term.
- Lease settings per property (property.lease_settings): repair deductible (only allowed for single-family homes and duplexes, 83.51(1)), pest control (resident or landlord; landlord by default in larger buildings, 83.51(2)(a)), lawn care and irrigation (houses), utilities paid by the landlord, keys, utility providers, where deposits are held (83.49(2)), and the owner's flood (83.512) and lead-paint answers. Every lease at the property fills in from them.
- Lease is final (approved by Wesley Owen, Nov 1, 2026): the residential lease, addenda and Florida disclosures are used as built from Florida ch. 83, without separate attorney review. The lease screens show the document only (no review notes or open questions). Settled with it: the owning LLC is Landlord and SIM REI is its agent (83.50); 60-day end-of-term notice with one month's rent as liquidated damages (83.575), so the office sends the 83.575(2) reminder before the notice window opens; early termination at 2x rent (83.595(4)); Obligo section 10 keeps the 83.491 fee-in-lieu notice and disclosure; the flood disclosure goes with every lease; abandoned property follows ch. 715 (no 83.67(5) legend); the welcome letter says the application fee is non-refundable.
- Renter's insurance (decided Nov 1, 2026): no LLIP. SIM REI's LLIP came through AppFolio and ends with it, so the lease no longer has the LLIP program, its monthly charge, or the landlord's right to buy coverage and bill the resident. Residents must carry their own policy (§1.6, §9) and show proof before move-in and at renewal; no proof is a lease breach. The landlord is listed as an interested party (additional interest) on the resident's policy, the same in §1.6, §9 and the welcome letter.
- Repair deductible and jury waiver (decided Nov 1, 2026): every single-family home lease carries a $150 resident repair deductible per incident (lease §3.2; Florida lets single-family and duplex leases shift some repair duties to the tenant, 83.51); not used on multi-unit buildings. The jury trial waiver (§4.3) stays.
- Lease expirations: at most 12% of units may end in any one month, for the portfolio and for each property (at least 1 per property). Avoid November, December and January. Leases end on the last day of a month. When offering a new lease or a renewal (from 90 days before the end), Parcel Manage recommends the length that keeps endings spread out: 12 months if that month is open, otherwise the closest length ending in an open month.
- Rent due on the 1st; late fee posts after the grace period per lease (SIM REI sample: $50 on the 6th). A lease can carry its own late-fee terms (days of grace and a flat amount or a percent of rent; imported from AppFolio for 12 SIM REI leases), which win over the company default. Florida residential law (ch. 83 Part II) sets no late-fee amount; the fee must be written into the lease (83.43 lets the lease call it rent). ACH is free to tenants; cards carry a convenience fee shown before confirming (SIM REI sample: 3%). Returned ACH posts an NSF fee and can block ACH after repeated returns. Online only (company setting rent.onlineOnly; SIM REI on, Wesley Nov 2, 2026): tenants pay only through the resident portal with Stripe (ACH or card); no Zelle, Cash App, cash or personal checks. The only exception is certified funds (money order or cashier's check), which the lease requires for move-in funds and after a returned payment; the office records those. Companies that turn it off also show check and money order instructions.
- Owners: every property belongs to one owner (the LLC or person on the deed, `entity`); an owner's properties are always grouped under it, and partners' shares stay on each property (`property_owner`). The Owners screen lists each owner with its properties, rent and estimated fee; "Put properties under this owner" moves properties to it.
- Management fees (decided Nov 1, 2026; set per company): the fee comes out of the owner's money under the signed management agreement, never from tenants, so Florida ch. 83 doesn't set it. Order: a property's own fee, else its owner's agreement, else the company default. Terms: monthly fee as a percent of rent collected (with an optional monthly minimum and maximum per property; Wesley, Nov 1, 2026), a flat amount per property, a flat amount per occupied unit, or none; whether the minimum or flat fee still applies when no rent comes in; a leasing fee and a renewal fee, each a percent of one month's rent, a flat amount, or none. All three can be set as the company default, per owner, or per property. Starting company default until SIM REI enters its own: 8% of rent collected, leasing fee 50% of one month's rent, no renewal fee. SIM REI (Wesley, Nov 1, 2026): it manages only its own properties, so the company default is 5% with no leasing or renewal fee; partnership deals (The Grove Tower's owner, 100 North Dewey St LLC) add a $500 leasing fee and a $250 renewal fee. Until payments run in Parcel Manage, screens show an estimate from current rents; the real fee posts from rent collected with owner statements.
- Automatic billing (Settings → Billing): off until the company picks the first month Parcel Manage bills (the cutover from the company's old software), so tenants are never billed twice. Then, daily: rent (with any scheduled increase) and recurring charges post on the due day; a late fee posts once on the late day if anything is still owed (the lease's own terms, else the company rule; commercial leases use their commercial terms), plus any daily fee the lease provides up to its cap. Each charge posts once.
- Books: each owner (LLC) has its own double-entry books on a standard chart of accounts. Charges debit receivables and credit income; rent payments debit operating cash; security deposits debit trust cash and credit deposits held, so trust always equals deposits held (83.49). A returned payment is reversed (never deleted) and the lease's returned-payment fee is charged if the lease has one. Manual journal entries post only when balanced; mistakes are reversed. Cutover posts the old software's balances (and deposits held) once per lease. Switching is product-neutral: each company names the software it is leaving (AppFolio, Buildium, Rent Manager, Yardi, a spreadsheet, …) on the switch-over checklist, and the checklist, cutover and notes use that name; SIM REI's is AppFolio.
- Owner distributions monthly, split by ownership %, after expenses, management fees, debt service and reserve. Closing a month for an owner posts the management fee from rent actually collected that month (and leasing fees for leases that started), saves the statement, and lists each payee's share: operating cash after fees, less unpaid approved bills and the owner's cash reserve (set per owner), never below zero. Partners are paid when every property of the owner has the same partners (e.g. The Grove Tower); otherwise the owner LLC is paid. Payouts are recorded when sent (ACH payouts come with bank connections). Statements e-mail as a PDF to the owner and partners.

## Commercial leases (Fla. Stat. ch. 83 Part I)
- Commercial units use SIM REI's commercial lease, rebuilt against Florida's nonresidential rules (approved by Wesley Owen, Nov 1, 2026). Residential rules (7-24 month planner, short-term surcharge, 83.49 deposits, 83.53 entry, flood and lead disclosures) don't apply.
- Term: 3, 4 or 5 years (default 3; Wesley, Nov 1, 2026). Per-lease terms (lease.commercial_terms): business name (dba), permitted use, yearly rent increase of 0%, 3% or 5% (default 5%, printed as a schedule), renewal option (default one 1-year term, or 2 years, 90 days' notice; renewal Base Rent is the last rent increased by 3% or 5%, default 5%), CAM fee (fixed or adjustable), pro rata utilities, late charge (default 10% of the late amount after the 6th, no compounding), returned payment fee ($50), repair threshold ($750 per occurrence), systems warranty (90 days), landlord's and tenant's work. Per building (lease settings): deposit bank and the legal description for Exhibit A, filled in from the county property appraiser when the property is saved (Lake County so far; the appraiser's text is abbreviated, so check it against the deed).
- Florida rules built into the lease: unpaid rent is a default only after a 3-day written notice (83.20(2)); possession only by court action, surrender or abandonment, never self-help (83.05); abandonment presumption (83.05(3)); holdover without written consent is a tenancy at sufferance with double rent (83.04, 83.06(1)); accepting all past-due rent waives that eviction (83.202); the statutory landlord's lien (83.08); the radon notice word for word (404.056(5)); 713.10 lien notice; no sales tax on commercial rent (repealed Oct 1, 2024); firearms locked in vehicles allowed (790.251).

## E-signature
- Leases are signed online under Florida's Electronic Signature Act (ch. 668, including the UETA at 668.50) and the federal E-SIGN Act. Sending freezes the filled documents and records their fingerprint, so what's signed can't change; every blank must be filled first. Tenants sign in order, then guarantors, then the landlord's agent. Each signer agrees to sign electronically, types their name exactly as on the lease, and adds initials; the time, IP address and device are logged. The signed PDF with a signature certificate is filed on the lease and e-mailed to everyone, and a draft lease becomes active.

## Florida notices (law — have the company's attorney review templates before first use)
- 3-day notice to pay or deliver possession (83.56(3)): rent only; deadline excludes Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays (the company counts the 11 federal holidays plus the day after Thanksgiving, a Florida legal holiday; Wesley, Nov 2026); +5 days if mailed; hand delivery or posting if absent.
- 7-day notices of noncompliance with/without opportunity to cure (83.56(2)).
- Notice of entry for repairs: at least 24 hours, entry between 7:30 AM and 8:00 PM (83.53(2), as amended in 2022).
- Security deposit: return within 15 days, or send notice of intent to claim by certified mail within 30 days; tenant has 15 days to object (83.49). The move-out screen records the move-out (ends the lease, frees the unit), lists deductions, shows each 83.49(3) deadline with days left, prefills the statutory claim notice, won't deduct until the objection window has closed (15 days after receipt; certified mail assumed received in 5 days), then applies the deposit to what's owed and records the refund, all posted from trust. The dashboard lists every open deposit deadline.
- Legal notices are **never sent automatically**; they're drafted for approval. Parcel Manage builds each one from the statutory form, a person reviews and sends it, and the delivery method is recorded (posted, hand-delivered, mailed, certified mail, or e-mail only when the tenant signed the 83.505 e-mail addendum, 83.56(4)). Every letter is filed as a PDF on the tenant's record. The 3-day deadline excludes the delivery day, weekends and those holidays (plus 5 days if mailed).
