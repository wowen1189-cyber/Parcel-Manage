# SIM REI Residential Lease: Review Against Florida Statutes Chapter 83, Part II

**Prepared for:** SIM REI Property Management LLC (agent for the owning LLCs), Eustis, FL (Lake County)
**Documents reviewed:** `Residential Lease.pdf` (14 pages, AppFolio template with addenda 6–12), `Flood Disclosure.pdf` (1 page), `Lead Based Paint Disclosure Pre 1978.pdf` (1 page)
**Statute used:** Chapter 83, 2025 Florida Statutes, as saved from flsenate.gov (`docs/reference/florida-chapter-83-landlord-tenant-2025.html`)
**Review date:** 2026-11-01

> **This is not legal advice.** It is a factual comparison of the template text with the saved statute text, written for the owner and the owner's attorney. Statute quotes come only from the saved chapter 83 file. Anything governed by other law (other Florida chapters, federal law, case law) is marked **"outside the saved statute"**, and this review makes no claim about what that law says. Every suggested wording is **suggested, attorney to approve**.

How to read the classes:
- **Conflicts with the statute (must change):** the clause says something different from what the saved statute requires, or depends on a statutory condition the template does not meet.
- **Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review):** the clause may be void under 83.47 or 83.45, is inconsistent with other parts of the lease, or depends on law outside the saved statute.
- **Missing or incomplete required disclosure:** the statute requires a disclosure that is missing or incomplete.
- **OK as written:** checked against the statute, no change needed (some have optional notes).

### Top 5 must-fix items

1. **Security deposit timing and statutory disclosure (§1.4, §3.5 "Deposit Refund"; 83.49(2)(d), 83.49(3)(a)).** The lease says the deposit or an accounting will be returned "not more than thirty (30) days" after the term ends. The statute requires return **within 15 days** if there is no claim. If there is a claim, it requires a **written notice of intent to claim within 30 days**, sent by certified mail or by e-mail under 83.505. The all-caps deposit disclosure is an older wording ("MUST MAIL RESIDENT NOTICE") and does not match the text 83.49(2)(d) now requires ("MUST PROVIDE YOU WRITTEN NOTICE IN PERSON, BY MAIL, OR BY E-MAIL IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 83.505"). The depository notice also leaves out the bank's address and whether the tenant is entitled to interest.
2. **Entry notice (§3.4; 83.53(2)).** The lease says Florida "generally requires at least 12 hours' notice." The statute says reasonable notice for repair "is notice given at least 24 hours prior to the entry", with entry between 7:30 a.m. and 8:00 p.m.
3. **60-day move-out notice and one month's rent "liquidated damages" (§3.5; 83.575(1)–(2)).** The tenant-notice clause is allowed only "if such provision also requires the landlord to notify the tenant ... if the rental agreement will not be renewed", and the lease has no such landlord duty. Liquidated damages for missing the notice are allowed only if the landlord first sends a written notice of the tenant's obligations, delivered under 83.56(4) "within 15 days before the start of the notification period". The lease has no such step and also charges for failing to give notice "to renew".
4. **Electronic notices (§4.2 last paragraph / §4.4; 83.505(1)).** E-mail delivery of notices required by ch. 83 (3-day notices, deposit claims, and others) needs a **separate signed addendum** in substantially the statutory form, with each party's e-mail address and a statement that the election is voluntary and can be revoked. A general consent sentence inside the lease does not meet this requirement.
5. **"Other Remedies": immediate forfeiture and "guilty of a misdemeanor" (§4.2; 83.56(3)–(4), 83.59(3)).** The clause says a late tenant "immediately forfeit[s] all rights to occupy" and is "guilty of a misdemeanor". The saved statute requires a 3-day demand, excluding weekends and court holidays. That notice requirement "may not be waived in the rental agreement", and possession is recovered only through the 83.59(3) routes. Nothing in Part II makes holding over a misdemeanor. Related must-fix items: the **military clause** (§4.1) is narrower than 83.682, which "may not be waived or modified". The **24-hour pet-removal default** (§2.4) skips the 7-day cure notice in 83.56(2)(b). The **mold release** (§3.2) is the kind of clause 83.47(1)(b) makes void.

### §1.1 Parties and Occupants / letterhead / §12 Sign page

**F1. Landlord name and address for notices (83.50).** *Missing or incomplete required disclosure*
- Lease: "and us, the owner/agent: <<Company Name>>". The SIM REI address appears only in the letterhead. The §12 signature block says only "Lessor".
- Statute 83.50: the landlord or agent "shall disclose in writing to the tenant, at or before the commencement of the tenancy, the name and address of the landlord or a person authorized to receive notices and demands in the landlord's behalf."
- Note: The template never states who is authorized to receive notices and demands, or at what address. <<Company Name>> may print SIM REI (the agent) and not the owner LLC, so the lease may also never name the owning landlord. The address matters elsewhere: the tenant's objection to a deposit claim "must be sent to (landlord's address)" (83.49(3)(a)), and tenant notices under 83.60(1)(b) may go to "the landlord's representative as designated pursuant to s. 83.50."
- Suggested wording (suggested, attorney to approve): "**Landlord and Notice Address.** The owner of the Premises is <<Owner Legal Name>>. SIM REI Property Management LLC, 432 N Eustis St, Eustis, FL 32726, is the owner's agent and is authorized to receive notices and demands on the owner's behalf. This authority continues until Landlord notifies you otherwise in writing." Signature block: "<<Owner Legal Name>>, by SIM REI Property Management LLC, its authorized agent. By: ____ Name/Title: ____ Date: ____".

**F2. Occupants (7-day guest rule).** *OK as written.* Chapter 83 does not regulate guest-stay limits.

### §1.2 Lease Duration (month-to-month conversion)

**F3. Termination notice during month-to-month (83.57(3), 83.56(4)).** *Conflicts with the statute (must change)*
- Lease: "Resident must provide at least 30 days' written notice ... no later than 30 days prior to the desired move-out date" and "Landlord may also terminate ... by providing at least 30 days' written notice, unless a longer period is required by law."
- Statute 83.57: a month-to-month tenancy may be terminated "by giving not less than 30 days' notice prior to the end of any monthly period", by "written notice in the manner provided in s. 83.56(4)."
- Note: The lease ties notice to any "desired move-out date", but the statute ties it to the **end of a monthly period**. A landlord notice that ends mid-month, or one delivered by a method not listed in 83.56(4), would not follow 83.57.
- Suggested wording: "During a month-to-month tenancy, either party may end the tenancy by giving the other written notice at least 30 days before the end of a monthly rental period. The tenancy then ends on the last day of that period. Notice must be delivered by mail, by hand delivery, by e-mail if the parties have signed the Electronic Notice Addendum (s. 83.505), or, if the Resident is absent, by leaving a copy at the residence (s. 83.56(4))."

**F4. Automatic $150 rent increase on conversion.** *OK as written.* The statute in the saved file sets no cap on rent amounts, and 83.425 preempts local regulation of "terms and conditions of rental agreements". The increase is disclosed in the signed lease, so the tenant agrees to it at signing. Optional: make the amount a setting (see Merge fields).

**F5. Unilateral changes to terms on 30 days' notice during month-to-month (§1.2 item 4).** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Lease: "Landlord reserves the right to modify the terms of this Lease with at least 30 days' written notice, including ... rent, fees, or property rules".
- Statute: Part II has no procedure for one-sided mid-tenancy changes. The tools it provides are ending a periodic tenancy under 83.57 and the good-faith duty in 83.44 ("Every rental agreement ... imposes an obligation of good faith"). Rule changes are enforceable only as "reasonable rules or regulations" (83.56(2)).
- Suggested wording: "During a month-to-month tenancy, Landlord may change the rent or other terms only by giving written notice at least 30 days before the end of a monthly rental period. The change takes effect on the first day of the next period. If Resident does not accept the change, Resident may end the tenancy under the notice terms above."

### §1.3 Rents and Charges

**F6. One-sided right to change the lease during the fixed term.** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Lease: "We may change the terms of this lease in accordance with applicable law, including rent increases and other modifications".
- Statute: nothing in Part II allows the landlord to change a fixed-term agreement alone. 83.44 requires good faith, and 83.45 allows a court to refuse to enforce unconscionable provisions.
- Suggested wording: "The rent and terms of this Lease are fixed for the Lease Term and may be changed only by a written amendment signed by Resident and Landlord."

**F7. Due date, grace, late fees, proration.** *OK as written.* Rent is due on the 1st and late after the 3rd. The <<Late Fee Rule>> applies, then <<Daily Late Fee>> "beginning after the 4th". Chapter 83 in the saved file sets no late-fee amount. 83.46(1) says rent "is uniformly apportionable from day to day", which matches the proration. Note: "late after the 3rd" and the daily fee "beginning after the 4th" should be checked against the settings so the two dates line up.

**F8. Fees designated as "Additional Rent".** *OK as written.* 83.43(12): "Rent" includes "any other payments due the landlord from the tenant as may be designated as rent in a written rental agreement." Whether fees designated as rent can go in a 3-day notice is case law, outside the saved statute. Attorney to confirm before the app puts fees on 3-day notices.

**F9. $50 NSF fee; returned checks to law enforcement (§1.3 and §4.2).** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Lease: "A fee of $50.00 shall be applied by Landlord to any dishonored check". Also: "We may turn any returned checks over to law enforcement officials for prosecution according to law."
- Statute: dishonored-check charges and prosecution are outside the saved statute (ss. 68.065 / 832.08 are not in the file). Attorney to confirm the allowed amount and wording. This review does not state a cap.
- Suggested wording: "A returned-payment fee of $<<NSF Fee>> (not more than the amount allowed by Florida law) applies to any dishonored check or electronic payment."

### §1.4 Security Deposit (and §3.5 "Deposit Refund")

**F10. Return timing (no claim within 15 days, claim notice within 30 days).** *Conflicts with the statute (must change)*
- Lease §1.4: "Any amount remaining ... together with a written accounting ... will be returned by mail to Resident not more than thirty (30) days after expiration of the Term."
- Statute 83.49(3)(a): "if the landlord does not intend to impose a claim ... the landlord must return the security deposit ... within 15 days after the termination of the rental agreement. If the landlord intends to impose a claim ... the landlord must, within 30 days ..., provide the tenant written notice by certified mail ... or by e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505". Under 83.49(3)(b), after the 15-day objection window the landlord "shall remit the balance ... within 30 days after the date of the notice".
- Note: The lease gives one 30-day deadline for everything and allows plain mail. If the notice is late, the statute says the landlord "forfeits the right to impose a claim upon the security deposit". The claim notice must use the 83.49(3)(a) form: "This is a notice of my intention to impose a claim for damages in the amount of ___ upon your security deposit, due to ___. ..." The app should generate it word for word.
- Suggested wording: "**Return of Deposit.** If Landlord does not intend to make a claim on the deposit, Landlord will return the full deposit (with any interest due) within 15 days after the Lease ends and you vacate. If Landlord intends to make a claim, Landlord will send you written notice of the claim and the reason for it within 30 days after the Lease ends. Landlord will send it by certified mail to your last known mailing address, or by e-mail if you have signed the Electronic Notice Addendum. You have 15 days after you receive that notice to object in writing at the Landlord's notice address. Landlord will send any remaining balance within 30 days after the date of the notice. Please give Landlord your forwarding address in writing when you move out."

**F11. §3.5 "Deposit Refund" repeats the 30-day rule.** *Conflicts with the statute (must change)*
- Lease §3.5: "We'll mail ... your security-deposit refund ... no later than 30 (thirty) days after the Lease Contract termination".
- Statute: same as F10 (83.49(3)(a)–(b)).
- Suggested wording: replace with "Landlord will return or make a claim on your security deposit as described in Section 1.4 and section 83.49(3), Florida Statutes."

**F12. All-caps statutory deposit disclosure does not match current 83.49(2)(d).** *Conflicts with the statute (must change)*
- Lease: "THE LANDLORD MUST MAIL RESIDENT NOTICE, WITHIN 30 DAYS AFTER RESIDENT MOVE OUT" and "IF THE LANDLORD FAILS TO TIMELY MAIL RESIDENT NOTICE".
- Statute 83.49(2)(d): the notice must "Contain the following disclosure", which reads in part: "THE LANDLORD MUST PROVIDE YOU WRITTEN NOTICE IN PERSON, BY MAIL, OR BY E-MAIL IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 83.505, FLORIDA STATUTES, WITHIN 30 DAYS AFTER YOU MOVE OUT" and "IF THE LANDLORD FAILS TO TIMELY PROVIDE YOU NOTICE".
- Note: The template uses the older "MAIL" wording and also swaps "YOU/YOUR" for "RESIDENT" ("GIVE THE LANDLORD RESIDENT NEW ADDRESS", "RESIDENT MOVE OUT"). That leaves grammatical errors in a block the statute prescribes word for word. 83.49(2)(d) does not use "substantially" for this text, so use it verbatim.
- Suggested wording (verbatim from 83.49(2)(d)):
  > YOUR RENTAL AGREEMENT REQUIRES PAYMENT OF CERTAIN DEPOSITS. THE LANDLORD MAY TRANSFER ADVANCE RENTS TO THE LANDLORD'S ACCOUNT AS THEY ARE DUE AND WITHOUT NOTICE. WHEN YOU MOVE OUT, YOU MUST GIVE THE LANDLORD YOUR NEW ADDRESS SO THAT THE LANDLORD CAN SEND YOU NOTICES REGARDING YOUR DEPOSIT. THE LANDLORD MUST PROVIDE YOU WRITTEN NOTICE IN PERSON, BY MAIL, OR BY E-MAIL IN ACCORDANCE WITH SECTION 83.505, FLORIDA STATUTES, WITHIN 30 DAYS AFTER YOU MOVE OUT, OF THE LANDLORD'S INTENT TO IMPOSE A CLAIM AGAINST THE DEPOSIT. IF YOU DO NOT REPLY TO THE LANDLORD STATING YOUR OBJECTION TO THE CLAIM WITHIN 15 DAYS AFTER RECEIPT OF THE LANDLORD'S WRITTEN NOTICE, THE LANDLORD WILL COLLECT THE CLAIM AND MUST MAIL YOU THE REMAINING DEPOSIT, IF ANY.
  >
  > IF THE LANDLORD FAILS TO TIMELY PROVIDE YOU NOTICE, THE LANDLORD MUST RETURN THE DEPOSIT BUT MAY LATER FILE A LAWSUIT AGAINST YOU FOR DAMAGES. IF YOU FAIL TO TIMELY OBJECT TO A CLAIM, THE LANDLORD MAY COLLECT FROM THE DEPOSIT, BUT YOU MAY LATER FILE A LAWSUIT CLAIMING A REFUND.
  >
  > YOU SHOULD ATTEMPT TO INFORMALLY RESOLVE ANY DISPUTE BEFORE FILING A LAWSUIT. GENERALLY, THE PARTY IN WHOSE FAVOR A JUDGMENT IS RENDERED WILL BE AWARDED COSTS AND ATTORNEY FEES PAYABLE BY THE LOSING PARTY.
  >
  > THIS DISCLOSURE IS BASIC. PLEASE REFER TO PART II OF CHAPTER 83, FLORIDA STATUTES, TO DETERMINE YOUR LEGAL RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS.

**F13. Depository notice: bank checkboxes, missing address, missing interest statement.** *Missing or incomplete required disclosure*
- Lease: "Resident's security deposit will be held in an account at: ☐ Chase bank ☐ United Southern Bank".
- Statute 83.49(2): the notice must "(b) State the name and address of the depository where the advance rent or security deposit is being held or state that the landlord has posted a surety bond" and "(c) State whether the tenant is entitled to interest on the deposit." Under 83.49(1)(a)/(b) the account must be "a separate non-interest-bearing account" or "a separate interest-bearing account in a Florida financial institution". If interest-bearing, the tenant receives "at least 75 percent of the annualized average interest rate ... or interest at the rate of 5 percent per year". (Subsection (2) "does not apply to any landlord who rents fewer than five individual dwelling units.")
- Note: There is no depository address and no interest statement. The template does not say whether the account is interest-bearing or non-interest-bearing, or whether a surety bond is used. If no box is checked, nothing is disclosed. If the depository changes later, the tenant must be notified "within 30 days after the change."
- Suggested wording: "Your security deposit and any advance rent are held in a separate ☐ non-interest-bearing ☐ interest-bearing account at <<Deposit Bank Name>>, <<Deposit Bank Address>>, a Florida financial institution. ☐ You are not entitled to interest on the deposit. ☐ You are entitled to interest of <<Interest Terms>>, paid or credited at least once a year. If Landlord changes where or how the deposit is held, Landlord will notify you in writing within 30 days of the change." (App: make bank name, address, and account type a per-owner setting, not a printed checkbox.)

**F14. What the deposit secures; no use as last month's rent; joint residents.** *OK as written.* This is consistent with 83.43(14) ("moneys held by the landlord as security for the performance of the rental agreement"). All deductions still go through the 83.49(3) notice process.

### §1.5 Utilities

**F15. Discontinued association or landlord services "shall not be construed as a prohibited practice".** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Lease: "The discontinuation of any such services by the ... landlord shall not be construed as a prohibited practice nor shall it constitute a default under the lease."
- Statute 83.67(1): a landlord "shall not cause, directly or indirectly, the termination or interruption of any utility service furnished the tenant, including ... water, ... garbage collection ... whether or not the utility service is under the control of, or payment is made by, the landlord." 83.47(1)(a) voids provisions that "waive or preclude the rights, remedies, or requirements set forth in this part."
- Suggested wording: "If a condominium or homeowners association stops providing a service it currently provides (such as cable, internet, alarm monitoring, or a guarded gate), Landlord is not required to replace that service. Landlord will not cause any utility service to be terminated or interrupted, as provided in s. 83.67, Florida Statutes. If an essential utility (water, sewer, or trash) must be moved into Resident's name, Landlord will give Resident reasonable written notice before the change."

### §1.6 Insurance

**F16. "You assume all liability for personal injury, property damage or loss, and insurable risk."** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Statute 83.47(1)(b): a provision is "void and unenforceable to the extent that it ... Purports to limit or preclude any liability of the landlord to the tenant ... arising under law."
- Suggested wording: "Landlord's insurance does not cover your personal property or your liability. We strongly encourage you to carry renter's insurance. This section does not limit any liability Landlord has to you under law."

**F17. Insurance requirements contradict each other (§1.3, §1.6, §9, §11).** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Lease §1.6: renter's policy with $100,000 liability that "shall name Landlord as an additional insured", and lapse "shall be a Default". §1.3 and §9: if no proof is given, the LLIP charge applies. §11: "add additional interest of 'SIM REI Property Management, LLC'".
- Statute: insurance requirements are outside the saved statute (Part II mentions tenant insurance only for flotation beds in 83.535).
- Note: The lease requires both "additional insured" and "additional interest", and treats a lapse as both a default and an LLIP trigger. The tenant cannot tell which rule applies. Pick one model.
- Suggested wording: "Resident must maintain liability coverage of at least $<<Required Liability Limit>> for damage to Landlord's property, as described in the Required Insurance Addendum. Resident may meet this requirement with Resident's own policy listing <<Owner Legal Name>> c/o SIM REI Property Management LLC as an interested party, or through Landlord's LLIP program at $<<LLIP Monthly Cost>> per month."

### §1.7 Keys and Locks

**F18. Keys list, lock changes, $125 lockout charge.** *OK as written.* This does not conflict with 83.67(2), which concerns the landlord preventing access. Make "Front door" and $125 settings.

### §2 Policies and Procedures

**F19. §2.1 Rules effective immediately.** *OK as written.* Rule enforcement is limited to "reasonable rules or regulations" (83.56(2)). Optional: say rules must be reasonable and given to the resident in writing.

**F20. §2.2 Casualty Loss: "We're not liable ... from any cause ... unless otherwise required by law."** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Statute 83.47(1)(b) (quoted in F16). Also 83.63: if the premises are damaged other than by the tenant "so that the enjoyment of the premises is substantially impaired", the tenant may terminate or get rent reduced.
- Note: The savings clause helps, but "from any cause" is the kind of blanket limit 83.47(1)(b) voids.
- Suggested wording: "Landlord is not responsible for loss of or damage to Resident's personal property caused by fire, flood, storms, utility interruption, theft, or vandalism, except where caused by Landlord's negligence or failure to perform a duty under law, including s. 83.51. If the Premises are damaged by casualty, Resident has the rights stated in s. 83.63, Florida Statutes."

**F21. §2.2 Crime: "Unless otherwise provided by law, we're not liable ... caused by criminal conduct of other persons".** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*. Same basis as F20 (83.47(1)(b)). Suggested wording: "Landlord does not guarantee security and is not responsible for crimes committed by others, except to the extent Landlord is liable under law."

**F22. §2.2 Smoke detectors, crime/drug free, smoke free, use of premises.** *OK as written.* Consistent with 83.52(5)–(7) and 83.51(2)(b) (landlord installs smoke detection devices "at the commencement of the tenancy of a single-family home or duplex").

**F23. §2.3 Parking, stored and abandoned vehicles, towing.** *OK as written.* Towing under s. 715.07 is outside the saved statute. Note: under 83.56(2)(b), "unauthorized ... vehicles; parking in an unauthorized manner" is curable noncompliance that needs a 7-day notice to cure before termination.

**F24. §2.4 Pets: "You must remove an illegal animal within 24 hours of notice from us, or you will be considered in default".** *Conflicts with the statute (must change)*
- Statute 83.56(2)(b) lists "having or permitting unauthorized pets" as noncompliance where the tenant "should be given an opportunity to cure", with a notice to correct "within 7 days". 83.56(4): "The notice requirements of subsections (1)-(3) may not be waived in the rental agreement."
- Suggested wording: "Unauthorized animals are a lease violation. If an unauthorized animal is found, Landlord may deliver a 7-day notice to cure under s. 83.56(2)(b), Florida Statutes. If the animal is not removed within 7 days after delivery, Landlord may terminate the Lease. Pet-related charges may also apply."

**F25. §2.4 Service animals: "We may require a written statement from a qualified professional".** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*. Outside the saved statute: assistance and service animals are governed by the federal Fair Housing Act and Fla. Stat. 413.08 / 760.27, which are not in the file. Attorney to supply wording. Note that the template uses "service animal" only. The pet addendum's rent, fee, and "presumed strays" terms (F61) should state that they do not apply to assistance animals.

**F26. §2.4 De-fleaing/deodorizing charge whenever an animal has been in the unit.** *OK as written* under ch. 83 (a damages-type charge). Attorney to confirm how it applies to assistance animals (outside the saved statute).

### §3 Responsibilities

**F27. §3.1 "You accept the apartment ... as is ... We disclaim all implied warranties."** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Statute 83.51(1): landlord duties to comply with codes or maintain structure and plumbing; "The landlord's obligations under this subsection may be altered or modified in writing with respect to a single-family home or duplex." 83.47(1)(a) voids waivers of Part II rights.
- Note: For units other than single-family homes and duplexes, 83.51(1) duties cannot be shifted. An "as is" clause also conflicts with 83.51(1)(b), which says the landlord "must ensure that screens are installed in a reasonable condition" at commencement.
- Suggested wording: "Resident has inspected the Premises and accepts its condition, subject to Landlord's continuing duties under s. 83.51, Florida Statutes. Resident will keep the Premises clean and in good condition as required by s. 83.52."

**F28. §3.2 Plumbing misuse, HVAC filters, smoke detector testing, water filters.** *OK as written.* Consistent with 83.52(4)–(5) and 83.51(4) ("The landlord is not responsible ... for conditions created or caused by the negligent or wrongful act or omission of the tenant").

**F29. §3.2 Resident Repair Deductible ($150 per incident; checkbox).** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Lease: "Resident agrees to pay the first $150.00 of any repair or maintenance cost per incident, unless otherwise required to be covered solely by the Landlord under Florida law."
- Statute 83.51(1) (only single-family homes or duplexes may modify landlord duties in writing); 83.47(1)(a)–(b).
- Note: For multifamily units, a deductible on 83.51(1) repairs (structure, plumbing, code items) shifts a landlord duty the statute does not allow to be shifted. "Major system failures" is undefined. The checkbox makes it optional per lease, which helps.
- Suggested wording: "**(Single-family homes and duplexes only, if checked.)** Resident will pay the first $<<Repair Deductible>> of each repair that is not caused by Landlord's negligence and is not a repair Landlord must make under s. 83.51(1) (roof, windows, doors, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls, foundation, other structural parts, plumbing, and building/housing/health code items). This deductible does not apply to units in buildings with three or more units."

**F30. §3.2 Damage due to negligence (100% tenant cost).** *OK as written.* Consistent with 83.51(4) and 83.52(6). Optional: "Failure to report issues in a timely manner" should be limited to damage caused by the delay.

**F31. §3.2 Mold & Mildew release.** *Conflicts with the statute (must change)*
- Lease: "Resident relieves Landlord from any liability for any bodily injury or damages to property caused by or associated with moisture or the growth of or occurrence of mold or mildew".
- Statute 83.47(1)(b): void "to the extent that it ... Purports to limit or preclude any liability of the landlord to the tenant ... arising under law."
- Note: Unlike F20 and F21, this clause has no "unless required by law" limit. The prevention and reporting duties in the clause are fine.
- Suggested wording: delete the release sentence and keep the rest. Optionally add: "Resident is responsible for mold or moisture damage caused by Resident's failure to ventilate, clean, or promptly report leaks or moisture. Landlord remains responsible for conditions it must repair under s. 83.51."

**F32. §3.2 Pest control by Resident after 30 days.** *OK as written.* For single-family homes and duplexes, 83.51(2)(a) does not apply. For other units, the landlord's extermination duty applies "Unless otherwise agreed in writing", and this lease is that written agreement. Bed bugs: see F56.

**F33. §3.2 Lawn and irrigation; Lawn Maintenance Assignment checkbox; §3.6 Single-family home maintenance.** *OK as written.* 83.51(1) may be "altered or modified in writing with respect to a single-family home or duplex", and 83.51(2) applies "Unless otherwise agreed in writing". The checkbox and the "single-family homes only" scope keep this conditional. HOA fines charged back are a damages item.

**F34. §3.3 Report within 24 hours; written notice via portal or e-mail.** *OK as written.* Note: the tenant's statutory notices under 83.56(1) and 83.60(1)(b) are 7-day written notices, and the lease cannot restrict how they are delivered (83.56(4)). Portal or e-mail as a convenience is fine.

**F35. §3.3 "We reserve the right to turn off equipment and interrupt utility services as necessary".** *Likely unenforceable or risky (attorney to review)*
- Statute 83.67(1) (quoted in F15). Penalty: 83.67(6), "actual and consequential damages or 3 months' rent, whichever is greater".

### D1. Flood Disclosure (83.512). *OK as written* (minor recommendations)

The flood disclosure must be in a separate document." It must be "in substantially the following form".

The landlord is "the owner or lessor" (83.43(9)), so the **owner LLC** should answer items 1–3. Print the owner's legal name with "by SIM REI Property Management LLC, agent".

## 5. Open questions for the owner

1. **Who is "Landlord" on each lease?** Should the documents name the owning LLC as landlord, "by SIM REI Property Management LLC, agent"? This affects 83.50, the flood disclosure ("Landlord's ownership"), and the signature block.
2. **Deposit accounts:** For each owner LLC, which bank holds deposits (name and **address**)? Is the account interest-bearing or non-interest-bearing, or does the owner use a surety bond (83.49(1)(c))? Does each owner (or SIM REI as agent) rent five or more units, which is the 83.49(2) threshold?
3. **Electronic notices:** Do you want to send 3-day notices, deposit claims, and other ch. 83 notices by e-mail? If so, a separate 83.505 addendum is needed, and SIM REI must also make a landlord election and designate an e-mail address.
4. **End-of-term notice:** Do you want to keep a 60-day notice and a liquidated-damages charge for no notice? If so, you must (a) commit to giving tenants the same notice of non-renewal and (b) send the 83.575(2) pre-notice within 15 days before the notice period starts. The app can schedule this.
5. **Early termination (§7):** Do you want to offer Choice 1 at 2 × rent? What notice period (≤60 days)? Will you move §7 to a separately signed page?
6. **Obligo:** What exactly does a tenant sign with Obligo (fee, insurance, surety, or installment plan)? Is the option offered to every new tenant at a property, or only to "eligible" applicants? This decides whether 83.491 applies and whether the "If you are eligible" wording is a problem under 83.491(7).
7. **Insurance model:** Do you want the §1.6 mandatory renter's policy naming the landlord as **additional insured**, or the §9 Required Insurance / LLIP model with the landlord as **interested party**? Pick one.
8. **Property mix:** Are any properties not single-family homes or duplexes? Several tenant-duty shifts (repair deductible, as-is clause, pest control, bed bugs) are allowed only for single-family/duplex units, or "unless otherwise agreed in writing" under 83.51(2).
9. **Personal property after move-out:** Do you want to add the 83.67(5) legend (no landlord liability for storage or disposition after surrender, abandonment, or death of the last tenant), or keep following ch. 715?
10. **Flood disclosure scope:** Use it on every lease, or only terms of 1 year or longer as 83.512(1) requires? Who answers items 1–3 for each property (the owner, not SIM REI)?
11. **Pre-1978 properties:** Which properties were built before 1978? Who signs the agent's acknowledgment for SIM REI?
12. **Items outside the saved statute for the attorney:** NSF fee amount and returned-check prosecution language (ss. 68.065 / 832.08); assistance and service animal policy (FHA, ss. 413.08 / 760.27); jury waiver; radon notice (s. 404.056(5)); e-signature consent (ch. 668); credit reporting; the "presumed strays" pet clause; LLIP placement rules; and whether any law supports the "misdemeanor" sentence (recommended for deletion in any case).
